# Scenario matrix

These examples clarify when a research bridge is useful.

| Scenario | Use the skill? | Behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| An unresolved coordination, attribution, incentive, network, or measurement problem with an actionable transfer | Yes | Generalize the mechanism, inspect direct evidence, and return a cited bridge with one practical test. |
| The user explicitly asks for cross-domain or literature-based discovery | Yes | Explore neighboring terminology, supporting and contradicting evidence, and transfer limits. |
| A paper suggests a reusable behavior or failure pattern | Yes | Offer it as an inactive motif with a plain hypothesis, observable pattern, and evidence boundary. |
| The outside field shares words but not a causal or structural relationship | No | Treat it as a surface analogy and omit it. |
| No decision, measurement, intervention, or test could change | No | Continue the main task without a research detour. |
| Routine implementation or settled documentation lookup | No | Complete the task without a research aside. |
| JSON-only, schema-only, receipt-only, or “return only” output | No | Preserve the requested output contract. |
| The context contains private identifiers | Only after safe generalization | Remove identifiers and reconstructable combinations before any request. |
| Only metadata is available | Lead only | Report bibliographic facts, not mechanism or transfer claims. |
| The same problem, mechanism, and source recur in one conversation | No repeat | Suppress the duplicate. |
| A source is inaccessible or its retraction status cannot be checked | Limited | Label it as unchecked or omit it; do not present it as supported evidence. |

## Compact positive example

```markdown
Research bridge

Problem: A detector's decisions may change how people behave next time.
Why this paper may help: Research on strategic classification studies people adapting to the rule used to score them.
Outside field: Strategic classification.
Paper, in plain English: Once people learn what a detector rewards or punishes, they may change visible behavior without changing the underlying risk.
Try this in your stack: In the detector-evaluation workflow, compare behavior before and after feedback rather than treating every post-feedback change as proof of manipulation.
First small test: Compare performance before and after users receive partial feedback.
Candidate motif: Feedback gaming; hypothesis: feedback changes visible behavior without changing underlying risk; observable pattern: measured risk falls after feedback while an independent outcome does not; evidence boundary: the paper suggests what to test but does not prove local manipulation; status: inactive.
Biggest caveat: The paper may assume that users know more about the detector than they do in the real system.
Evidence for the claim: [claim]; [source title]; [stable ID]; [direct article or abstract link]; [publication status]; [retrieved date]; [abstract or full text]; [bridge element]
```
